Mobile use, first look

Three directions for the Android app, as clickable mocks at the Galaxy Z Fold 7's real sizes. Nothing here is built yet. All content is made-up sample data. Tap rows, hours, days, tabs and switches; on direction A, drag across the coloured strip. Use the bar above to switch theme, folded or open screen, accent and density.

What to do: pick a direction (or parts of two) and answer the open questions at the bottom.

What we borrowed, and from whom

  • Google Maps Timeline. Places as visits with a start and end time ("Home, 12:00 to 9:05 am"), travel between them as its own row, and a day switcher with arrows.
  • Apple Screen Time and Digital Wellbeing. Screen time split by hour and coloured by category, a most-used apps list with time on the right, and notifications counted per sender.
  • Rewind and Limitless. One search box over everything you saw on screen, results show the matched words with app and time, and a strip you drag across the day.
  • Day One and Apple Journal. The day as a written entry first, a week strip with dots on days that have entries, and moments with a time, a place and a quote.
  • Daylio. A few highlights instead of a dashboard, so the day reads at a glance.

Proposed structure

Days
Home screen. Today opens first; arrows, the week strip or the calendar move to other days. Search across days sits at the top.
Chat
Ask about a day or tell the phone to do something. Uses Gemini Nano by default; Gemma 4 E4B or your own API key are one tap away.
Agents
Which agents are connected, how to connect a new one, what an agent is doing right now, and triggers. Triggers live here because they start agent routines.
Settings
Behind the shield button: permissions, what is collected, excluded apps, how long to keep data, delete a day. Per-app switches also show on each app's page.
Open screen
On the inner screen each tab becomes list and detail side by side (the Material 3 list-detail layout), with a navigation rail.

When an agent is acting, a dark bar with Stop sits at the top of every screen, matching the pill the phone already shows.

Direction A

Day strip

The day is one coloured strip from 6 am to midnight: where you were on top, what was on screen in the middle, notifications as dots. Drag across it to see any moment, tap to open that hour. Font is Google Sans Flex, bundled under the OFL; it is the Material 3 font, so it feels native on the Fold.

Optimises for scanning a whole day in one look and jumping to an exact time. It shows that the app knows when and where, which is the core of what's collected.

Weak spot is a dense strip on the cover screen: thin sessions under 5 minutes disappear, and the legend has to be learned once. The written summary is small next to the strip.

Direction B

Journal

The day reads like a journal page. Nano's summary is the headline in a reading serif, then highlights and moments with real quotes from your screen. There are no tabs: you ask about the day from the box at the bottom, and agents sit behind the plug button. Fonts are Literata for reading and Figtree for the interface, both OFL.

Optimises for the evening read on the open screen and for people who aren't tech nerds. It is the warmest and fewest concepts: one page per day, one box to ask.

Weak spot is that it leans on the summary being good, and Gemini Nano writes short and sometimes flat. Agents and triggers are one level deeper, so the "drive my phone" half of the app is less visible.

Direction C

Places

The day is a list of places like Google Maps Timeline, and everything else hangs off them: what you used at the office, who messaged you at the cafe, what you watched in the Uber. A greeting on top borrows Juno's warmth. Font is Rubik (OFL), slightly round and friendly.

Optimises for memory. People remember "the coffee with Arjun", not "2 pm", so places are the easiest way in. Four tabs make search, chat and agents equally visible.

Weak spot is days spent at home: one long visit holds everything and the timeline turns into a single block. It also depends on location being on.

Recommendation

Build A, the day strip, with B's reading layer on top of it. The strip is the one thing no other app shows, and it works in a quiet home day as well as a busy office one, where C's place list falls apart. On the cover screen it gives the day in one glance. From B, take the serif summary and the quoted moments for the inner screen's right pane. From C, take the greeting and the place names inside the strip (already there).

Taste calls I made

  1. Tabs. Three: Days, Chat, Agents. Triggers live inside Agents. Settings and privacy sit behind a shield button, not a tab.
  2. Colour. Each direction has its own muted accent (teal, ink blue, clay). None reuses Nano Chat's coral on peach, so the apps feel related but not the same. App categories use six low-chroma hues with a legend.
  3. Shapes. Bars and strips are straight with square ends on square-cornered panels. Buttons, the live bar and the composer stay round, like Nano Chat.
  4. App icons. Phosphor glyphs on soft round tints, not the real app logos (licensing, and they would make the screen loud). People get photos, groups get a people glyph.
  5. Summary voice. Second person, past tense, two or three sentences, names people and places. Always labelled as written on this phone by Gemini Nano.
  6. Agent control. Sending messages and buying things always asks first by default. Stop is on every screen while an agent acts.
  7. Numbers. Totals are a sentence ("6h 12m on screen, 79 notifications"), not big stat tiles.

What I'd like you to decide

  1. Direction. A (strip), B (journal), C (places), or A with B's reading layer as I recommend.
  2. Accent. Teal, ink blue, clay or moss. Try them with the swatches above.
  3. Where chat lives. Its own tab (A and C) or a box at the bottom of each day (B). The box makes chat feel like part of the day; the tab makes "tell the phone to do something" easier to find.
  4. Screen text. Show quoted text from your screens on the day page (B does), or only inside an hour or app and in search (A does). Quotes are more useful and more exposing if someone looks at your phone.
